## Discussed

Conversations about AI move quickly and come loaded with vocabulary. Words like token, hallucination, RAG and diffusion get used in lectures, YouTube tutorials and software marketing as if everyone already knows what they mean. In this session we stopped and asked what they actually mean, and why they matter to someone designing buildings rather than writing code.

We worked through a list of fourteen terms that kept coming up: machine learning, neural network, diffusion model, prompt, prompt engineering, hallucination, token, context window, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), fine-tuning, AI agent, large language model (LLM), training and multimodal AI.

A few points came through clearly. Knowing how a tool works changes how far you trust it. Once you understand that an LLM predicts likely text rather than looking facts up, hallucination stops being a mystery and checking your sources becomes an obvious habit. Once you understand that a diffusion model builds an image out of noise, it makes sense that it struggles with window mullions, stair geometry and lettering. And the difference between fine-tuning and RAG helps explain why some practice tools can quote a specification accurately while a general chatbot cannot.
