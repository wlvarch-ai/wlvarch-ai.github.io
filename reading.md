## Reports and professional bodies

- [**RIBA Artificial Intelligence Report 2026**](https://www.riba.org/work/insights-and-resources/ai-report/) — Royal Institute of British Architects
  RIBA's third annual AI report draws on its largest member survey to date, showing that 74% of practices now use AI on at least some projects, from early-stage visualisation through to project and practice management. It also sets out the profession's concerns, including environmental impact, intellectual property and the effect on the career path of those starting out, which makes it essential reading for anyone entering practice.
  

- [**AI and the Future of Architectural Education in the UK**](https://www.scosa.org.uk/_files/ugd/379c04_68f2ea1c9a9742b19f749584c6eb1e61.pdf) — SCOSA AI Working Group (September 2025)
  Produced by the Standing Conference of Schools of Architecture, this report combines a 2025 survey of students and staff with case studies of AI use in UK schools of architecture and in practices such as Foster + Partners and Arup. It recommends that AI literacy is built into the curriculum and taught critically, with attention to bias, provenance, ethics and authorship, so it is a direct guide to the skills and judgement you will be expected to show as an AI-enabled architect.
  

- [**AI in Commercial Property and Construction Report 2026**](https://www.rics.org/news-insights/ai-in-commercial-property-and-construction-report-2026) — RICS (August 2026)
  Based on more than 3,100 responses from surveyors and construction professionals worldwide, the report finds that around two-thirds of construction organisations now use AI in some form, up from just over half a year earlier, although most are still at pilot stage and a lack of skilled people remains the main barrier. It also raises a concern that recent graduates are using AI tools before they have the experience to judge what those tools produce, which is a direct argument for building critical judgement alongside technical skill while you are still studying.
  

- [**Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Surveying Practice**](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/conduct-competence/responsible-use-of-ai) — RICS Professional Standard (in effect from 9 March 2026)
  This is RICS's first mandatory professional standard on AI, setting out how members and regulated firms must govern data, assess AI tools before using them, check the reliability of outputs and be transparent with clients about where AI has been used. It shows how a professional body is turning ethical principles into enforceable practice, including the requirement that a named, qualified professional takes responsibility for any AI output that materially affects the service, a useful model for thinking about accountability in your own design work.
